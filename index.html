What's inside

A world, not just a website.

Most forum software is just threads and replies. Channels has a virtual world you can walk through: hub spaces with real characters, synchronized cinema rooms, a neighborhood with homes you can claim, and a shared canvas that the whole community draws on.

Privacy

What the server knows. And what it doesn't.

Channels was built on the assumption that a platform should collect as little about you as possible. This section states exactly what the server stores, how your IP is handled, and where the limits of that protection are.

What the server stores

Your generated username, an Argon2id hash of your passphrase, and an Argon2id hash of your passitems. The raw passphrase and passitems are deleted from the server the moment they are displayed to you during signup.

Content you create: threads, replies, pixels on the shared Paintit canvas, your avatar configuration. Home and neighborhood data if you claim a home.

A session token. Sessions are stored server-side and are deleted automatically when they expire.

The account contains nothing that connects to your real identity.

How IPs are handled

Your IP address is used for rate limiting: the server enforces limits on signup attempts, sign-in attempts, and WebSocket connections. These counters are held in server memory and are not written to your account record in the database.

The Caddy reverse proxy writes standard HTTP access logs, which include the IP address of each request. This is default web server behavior. Your IP is not linked to your account anywhere in the database.

What this protects
  • Your account cannot be traced back to an identity you use elsewhere. No email or phone number is required.
  • The site runs no third-party analytics, advertising networks, or tracking cookies.
  • Fonts are served from this server.
  • Content is served without any mechanism to identify you to other users by IP or device.
What this doesn't protect
  • Your ISP can see that you connect to channels.zone.
  • Caddy writes standard HTTP access logs, which include the IP of each request.
  • Cinema rooms embed YouTube videos. If you watch one, Google receives your IP and may set cookies.
How it works
1
You get credentials. Not a form to fill out.

A username is generated for you. Then a passphrase. Then 11 random words. The signup requires no personal information and takes about 30 seconds.

2
Write them down. There's no recovery.

Your credentials exist on your screen once, during signup. There is no "forgot my password" flow because there is no email address to reset to. If the credentials are lost, the account is gone. This is a feature, not an oversight.

3
You're in. The rest is up to you.

Browse the forum. Walk into a hub room. Claim a home in the neighborhood. Customize your avatar. Every account starts the same.

None of this was readable until you were inside. Threads, hub rooms, neighborhoods: all of it required an account. That decision protected users completely — and, as it turned out, made the site impossible to grow.

Why it ended

A charter that forbids revenue.

No revenue was possible by design. The site's own pillars were no ads, no algorithm, no investors, donations only. A product whose charter forbids revenue cannot be a business, and cannot be taken to investors without breaking the promise on its own front page.

Channels ran for six weeks on a VPS paid out of pocket. In August 2026 the bill stopped being worth paying for a site with no path to sustaining itself, and it was shut down.

Each of the decisions below was defensible in isolation. Together they made growth close to impossible. That tension — between protecting users completely and letting anyone discover you — is the thing this project actually taught me.

Zero organic users after launch. Not a marketing failure — structural:

Nothing was visible from outside.

All threads, hubs and neighborhood content sat behind the signup wall, so nothing was indexable, shareable, or evaluable before committing.

Signup was maximum-friction.

Generated credentials, 11 words to write on paper, no recovery ever — asked of a visitor who had not yet seen a single post.

Real-time social features need density.

Empty music stages and 1,800 empty homes are worse than not having the feature at all.
